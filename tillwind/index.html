01The moor

A hill of granite, gorse and 1,412 owners.

Penhallow Moor is common grazing above the village, with the Atlantic on one side and the old mine stacks on the other. The wind comes off the sea at an average of 7.9 metres a second, which is about as good as land in England gets.

In 2016 a dozen neighbours set up Tillwind to put that wind to work for the people who live under it. We raised £1.9 million from 640 people, and in 2019 Old Nell went up: one 2.3 MW turbine, named after the last pit pony at Wheal Penhallow. She has paid members' interest every year since, and she puts a share of every pound she earns into a fund that the village spends.

Now we want to add two more turbines on the same ridge, using the same road and the same grid connection, which is why this offer is cheaper per turbine than the first one.

09Honest risks

What could go wrong, in plain words.

01Less wind than we planned for

Our figures assume an average year. A still year like 2023 means lower interest, and a run of still years could mean none.

02Your money is not protected

Community shares are not covered by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme. Only put in what you could afford to lose.

03You can't take it out quickly

No withdrawals for three years, and after that only when the board agrees and the cash is there.

04Electricity prices fall

About half our output is sold at a fixed price until 2032. The rest goes up and down with the market.

Do I have to live in Cornwall to join?

No. Anyone over 16 living in the UK can buy shares. We give people in the parish the first two weeks of every offer, and 71% of members live within ten miles of the moor.

What happens to my shares when I die?

You can name who inherits them in the app. If you don't, they go to your estate and are paid out at the value you put in.

Will the turbines be noisy from the village?

The nearest home is 1.1 km from the closest new turbine. Our noise survey predicts 31 dB there in a strong south-westerly, about as loud as a quiet library.

What about birds and bats?

We surveyed for two years. The new turbines sit away from the cliff, where the choughs feed, and they slow down on still summer nights when bats are about.

Can I give shares as a present?

Yes. Buy them in someone else's name from £100 and we'll post a printed card with their member number.
